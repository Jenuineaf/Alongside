Forest green: can it carry Spark on its own?

The Feed screen, drawn twice at real phone size with real posts. Everything is identical between them except one thing: what colour Spark is. Plus the forest green I picked, and how success and error survive a green brand.

1 · The forest green

You asked me to pick the value. It's #1F4A34 — hue 149°, saturation 41%. That puts it in the same family as the sages (which run 67°–178°), just far deeper, and it is the only member of the set with enough chroma to hold a filled button.

It works in both directions, which is what a brand colour has to do: 10.06:1 as text on white and 10.06:1 as a fill under white text. Same number — that's the point of a value this deep. The lighter steps (#3F7A58, #2E6144) exist for hover and for large text; #8DB29C and paler are surfaces only.

2 · The two questions you asked

No — it can stay as it is

Does burnt orange #A8461B pass AA on white at text size?

Yes — 5.90:1, against the 4.5:1 that normal text needs. It does not need to go darker. It clears the bar even for a 12px spark count. The one place it needs help is orange text sitting on the pale orange "sparked" pill, where the tint eats contrast — there I use #8A3A14 (7.13:1). So: #C85A22 fills the star, #8A3A14 sets the count.

Both — but not for body copy

Does forest green work as body text, or only as a fill?

It genuinely works as both — 10.06:1 clears AAA in either direction, so there's no accessibility reason to avoid it. But I'd still keep post bodies and comments in sage-800 #3C403D (10.54:1) and spend the green on links, active nav, headings and button fills.

The reason isn't contrast, it's economics. If body text is green and links are green, links stop announcing themselves — you've spent the brand colour on the one element that didn't need it. Green has to mean "this does something". That only holds if the surrounding text isn't green.

One that did fail, and how I fixed it

My first pass put a forest-green tick on the dark confirmation toast in section 4. That's 1.52:1 — invisible. Dark green on a dark surface never works. The tick is now forest-300 #8DB29C at 6.54:1. Worth saying out loud because it's the trap with a deep brand colour: it's superb on white and useless on anything dark, so a dark surface needs the pale end of the ramp, not the brand value.

3 · Green means success — solving that

You're right that this is a real problem, and it's worse than it first looks. Conventional success green is #2E7D32. Against our brand that's 26° of hue apart and 1.96:1 in contrast — two greens close enough that neither can signal anything the other doesn't. A green toast on a green-branded app reads as chrome, not confirmation.

My recommendation: success stops being a green surface. Confirmation moves to a dark neutral toast with a green tick — the green becomes the mark of approval inside the message rather than the field it sits on. Nothing about a green brand makes a charcoal toast with a pale-green check ambiguous, and it reads instantly as "the app is answering you".

The second collision, which nobody mentions until it ships

Warning is conventionally amber — and conventional amber #B45309 is only 8° from Spark's ember. A warning banner would read as a giant Spark. So warning moves off orange onto amber-yellow #8A6100, which puts 24° and a large lightness gap between them. Error red #B3261E is 15° from ember, which is tighter than I'd like — but error is always an icon-plus-text banner in a form, never a filled pill in a card's action row, so they never appear in the same context. I'd watch it rather than solve it.

The rule that makes all of this safe

Every state is an icon plus words. Colour only ever reinforces. That was already the plan for colour-blind users, and here it earns its keep twice over — with a green brand and a green-adjacent success convention, red/green colour blindness is a bigger risk for us than for a blue app. Any state that would be ambiguous in greyscale is wrong, regardless of what the ratios say.

What I considered and rejected

The obvious alternative is a brighter success green, distinct from the deep brand. I don't recommend it: it puts two greens on screen that people must learn to tell apart, it weakens both, and the first time a success toast appears next to a green button the distinction collapses. Better to give success a different channel entirely than a slightly different shade. Your call, though — say the word and I'll mock the two-greens version so you can see it rather than take my word for it.

Why the ratio can't decide this for you — and why A is closer than you'd expect

I measured the sparked state against the unsparked state as they actually render. Option A scores 2.13. Option B scores 1.65. By the numbers A is the better design — the deep forest is a bigger luminance jump from the muted default than the ember is. A numbers-led process would pick A here, confidently, and I think it would be wrong.

It would be wrong because contrast ratio measures luminance and nothing else. What makes a control findable mid-scroll is whether it looks like a different kind of thing from its neighbours, and that's carried by hue and saturation. Scroll down to the two cards and the point makes itself: in A, the sparked Spark is a green pill sitting directly under a row of green role chips, beside green "Still looking" text, on a screen with a green nav button. It's perfectly legible — it just reads as one more tag rather than something you switched on. In B it's the only warm thing on the screen.

I'll be straight that the deep forest narrows this considerably. Against the earlier teal, A was genuinely hard to read; with #1F4A34 it isn't. A is a defensible choice now in a way it wasn't before. The case for B is no longer "you can't see it" — it's "you can't tell what it is."

6 · Your call

Both are honest options. A isn't a straw man — a single-family palette is calmer, more distinctive against every blue competitor in the category, and simpler to keep consistent. It costs you legibility on one control.

A · Sage only

Forest and sages, nothing else. Spark is forest green, one step off the primary button.

  • Calmest. Nothing on screen shouts.
  • No accent to misuse later.
  • Stronger than it was with the earlier teal — the deep forest is genuinely legible when sparked.
  • The cost: green is doing five jobs on one card — avatar, badges, role chips, "Still looking", and Spark. The sparked state reads as one more green pill rather than a control you toggled.

B · Sage + ember my recommendation

Mystic Forest everywhere, burnt orange on Spark and nothing else — the role split exactly as you proposed it.

  • The sparked state is unmistakable mid-scroll.
  • Ember stays scarce — once per screen — so it keeps its force.
  • Passes AA at 5.90:1 without going darker.
  • The cost: one rule to police forever — ember is never a button, never a link. The moment it lands on a CTA, Spark stops being special.
