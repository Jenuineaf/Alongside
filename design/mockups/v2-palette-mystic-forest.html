Mystic Forest: can sage carry Spark on its own?

The same Feed screen, the same three posts, drawn twice. The only thing that changes between them is what colour Spark is. Judge it at real size, on the device people will actually use.

The two questions you asked

No — it can stay as it is

Does burnt orange #A8461B pass AA on white at text size?

Yes — 5.90:1, against the 4.5:1 that normal-size text needs. It does not need to go darker. It clears the bar for a 12px spark count, and it's only 1.1 short of AAA. Keep #A8461B for the count and the filled icon; there's a darker #8A3A14 in the ramp for the one case that needs it — orange text sitting on the pale orange "sparked" pill, where the tint eats some contrast.

Fill and accent — not body

Does dark teal #3E5A63 work as body text?

Technically yes: 7.37:1 on white, which clears AAA. But passing isn't the same as right. I'd use it for links, active nav, headings and button fills — and set post bodies and comments in sage-900 #232624 (15.28:1) instead. Two reasons: a 200-word post body in saturated teal is tiring on a cheap LCD outdoors, and if body text and links are the same hue at similar weight, links stop announcing themselves. Reserving teal for "this is interactive" is what makes it work as a brand colour.

Your read, tested

You said Mystic Forest is a palette of neighbours — same saturation, similar value. Half of that is right, and the half that's wrong changes the reasoning, though not the conclusion.

Measured from the five hex values you gave me.
ColourHueSaturationLightnessOn white
#3E5A63 dark teal195°23%32%7.37
#62807F178°13%44%4.27
#8A9188 sage107°4%55%3.24
#A9AE85 olive67°20%60%2.31
#E0E0E0 light grey0°0%88%1.32
#A8461B burnt orange18°72%38%5.90

Not neighbours in hue. The five span 128° — teal to olive is a longer walk than teal to orange in the other direction. And the lightness spread is wide too, 32% to 88%.

Neighbours in saturation — and that's the whole problem. Every one of them sits between 4% and 23%. Nothing in the set is vivid. On a page built entirely from muted colour, a muted control cannot announce itself, because there's no chroma contrast left to spend. So your conclusion holds — nothing here can carry Spark — but the reason is chroma, not hue. That matters, because it rules out the tempting fix of just rotating to a different green.

The trap in the numbers

If you picked Spark's colour by contrast ratio alone, you'd pick the wrong one. Against the teal primary button, #62807F scores 1.73 and the orange scores 1.25 — the orange looks worse. But contrast ratio only measures luminance; it is blind to hue. #62807F is 17° from the primary and reads as the same colour slightly faded. The orange is 177° away — near-complementary — and separates instantly.

The sharpest version of this: I measured the sparked state against the unsparked state in both mockups as they actually render. Option A scores 1.56. Option B scores 1.65. By the numbers they are the same design. Look at the two panels above and they are obviously not. Every bit of the difference you can see is carried by hue and saturation — the things the ratio doesn't measure. That's why this decision has to be made with your eyes on a phone, and why I built it this way instead of just sending you a table.

Your call

Both are honest options — A is not a straw man. A single-family palette is calmer, more distinctive against every blue competitor, and easier to keep consistent. It costs you legibility on one control.

A · Sage only

Mystic Forest and nothing else. Spark is teal, one shade off the primary button.

  • Calmest. Nothing on screen shouts.
  • Nothing to get wrong later — no accent to misuse.
  • The cost: sparked and unsparked look nearly the same at a glance, and Spark competes with the primary button for the same visual slot.

B · Sage + ember my recommendation

Mystic Forest everywhere, burnt orange on Spark and nothing else — exactly the role split you proposed.

  • Sparked state is unmistakable while scrolling.
  • Orange stays scarce — one appearance per screen — so it keeps its force.
  • Passes AA at 5.90:1 without going darker.
  • The cost: one more colour to police. The rule has to be enforced: ember is never a button. The moment it lands on a CTA, Spark stops being special.

One thing I'd change in your proposal either way

You had teal doing brand, primary buttons, links and active nav. That's a lot of jobs for one value. In both mockups I've used teal-700 #3E5A63 for button fills and active nav, and kept body copy in sage-900 — otherwise the screen goes teal-on-teal and the button stops reading as the loudest thing. Same palette, just spread across the ramp instead of one hex doing everything.

Pick A or B and I'll write it into docs/design-system.md and build the shared components. Until then nothing is committed.
